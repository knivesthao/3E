# The Case for Literacy & English — Reusable Pitch Deck

> **App-agnostic.** This deck explains *why the problem matters* and *why English matters*, without referencing any specific product. Copy these sections into any similar app's pitch deck or presentation, in order, as slides.

---

## Slide 1 — The core problem: access, not ability

- Children in underserved communities don't fail to read because they can't learn — they fail because they have nothing to read.
- Books are costly to print and import, hard to ship to remote areas, and every new reading level or language requires another print run.
- The result is self-reinforcing: no books → no reading practice → weaker literacy → less demand for books.

---

## Slide 2 — The global literacy gap

- **70%** of 10-year-olds in low- and middle-income countries cannot read and understand a simple text.
- **739 million** adults worldwide lack basic literacy — **two-thirds of them are women**.
- **40%** of learners are not taught in a language they understand.

---

## Slide 3 — The Laos reality

- **~7.5 million** people, with **~2.3 million children** aged 0–14.
- Fewer than **1 in 7** children aged 7–14 have even **three books** at home.
- About **30%** of grade 2 students cannot read a single word.
- Roughly **1 in 5** young people are out of work, education, and training — a generation with few paths to skilled work.
- Per-child reading materials average **less than one book per year**.

---

## Slide 4 — Why English specifically

- English is the de facto language of the global economy, higher education, and technology.
- About **half of all websites** are in English.
- English proficiency is one of the strongest predictors of access to better jobs, scholarships, and remote work.
- For many families, learning English is the single most requested skill — yet classes and materials are scarce and expensive.

---

## Slide 5 — The infrastructure reality (phones > internet)

- **92%** of Lao households have a mobile phone.
- Only **1.7%** have home internet.
- Print can't reach remote learners — and always-online digital tools can't either.
- The gap isn't devices. It's content that works **offline, on the phone people already own**.

---

## Slide 6 — Who is left behind

- Children learning to read their own language.
- Children and adults learning English as a second language.
- Teachers with too few materials and too much preparation work.
- Local creators (writers, illustrators) with talent but no market or tools.
- Rural and low-income families for whom a physical book is a luxury.

---

## Slide 7 — The opportunity

- A large, underserved population that already owns the hardware to receive content.
- Documented demand for English instruction with almost no affordable supply.
- A chance to create paid, skilled work for local creators while closing the literacy gap.
- A model that scales by adding a language or a country — new content, not a rebuild.

---

## Slide 8 — The impact case

- Every learner gets materials matched to their reading level, in their own language.
- English learners get leveled content, so progress is measurable and consistent.
- Teachers get ready-made, leveled material with built-in support — less prep, less dependence on a single textbook.
- Local creators earn income from content the community actually needs.
